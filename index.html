// How it works

Project-based learning makes mentorship scalable.

Students move through guided, step-by-step modules at their own pace while instructors act as technical mentors — so nobody gets left behind and nobody gets bored.

Inspired by the project-based model of the Raspberry Pi Foundation Unity projects, adapted into a four-weekend journey with a single, personal game as the goal.
01

Guided Project

Modular lessons introduce core programming structures and engine tools.

02

Independent Build

Students apply their own ideas: custom assets, difficulty and levels.

03

Mentor Debugging

Instructors unblock problems and teach real troubleshooting workflows.

04

Playable Milestone

Testing locks in each stage before moving on to the next mechanic.

// FAQ

Questions? We've got answers.

Does my child need coding experience?

Not at all. The camp starts from a blank canvas. Guided modules introduce every concept step by step, and the mentor is there whenever a student gets stuck.

What software will students use?

Unity, one of the most popular game engines in the world, together with the C# programming language. Everything runs on Unity for Education licenses, so families don't pay for software.

Does my child need to bring a laptop?

No. Every student works at a fully configured desktop workstation in Computer Labs 1 & 2.

What is the schedule?

Saturdays and Sundays from 9:00 AM to 1:00 PM, for four consecutive weekends — 8 sessions and 32 guided hours in total.

What happens if my child misses a session?

Because lessons are modular and self-paced, students can catch up on the guided module and the mentor helps them get back on track.

Can we play the game at home?

Yes. On the final weekend each student exports a playable build of their game so they can share it with friends and family.

Can parents attend Demo Day?

Absolutely — Demo Day is designed for families. Parents play the finished games alongside their creators on the final Sunday.

How many students are in each cohort?

Seats are limited by the number of lab workstations. Final capacity is confirmed when the pilot cohort is approved.
